# Article Scope: The Drawings Show What We Designed. Who Records Why?

## Topic

Write about preserving the reasoning behind major infrastructure decisions—not merely the final drawings.

## Strong Angle

**“The Drawings Show What We Designed. Who Records Why?”**

Project records usually preserve the final alignment, equipment selection, materials, and details. They often do not preserve why one alternative was selected, which assumptions supported it, what risks were accepted, or what future condition would require reconsideration.

### Central Argument

> Infrastructure projects need a decision memory, not just a drawing archive.

This article connects engineering judgment, risk management, lifecycle reliability, and business leadership. It follows naturally from the recent writing on trusted advisors: a trusted advisor helps the client make a defensible decision and ensures the reasoning does not disappear when personnel change.

## Concise Outline

### 1. What Traditional Project Records Preserve

- Final plans, specifications, calculations, submittals, and meeting minutes
- Usually the selected solution, but not a concise explanation of the decision

### 2. What Gets Lost

- Alternatives considered
- Assumptions and incomplete information
- Owner preferences and operational constraints
- Risks consciously accepted
- Conditions that would invalidate the decision

### 3. Why This Matters Years Later

- Future engineers may “correct” an intentional design choice.
- Operations may use the system outside its evaluated conditions.
- Capital planners may repeat analysis already performed.
- A reasonable decision can look arbitrary without its original context.

### 4. What Belongs in a Decision Log

- Decision and date
- Alternatives considered
- Technical and operational basis
- Cost and schedule implications
- Assumptions and unresolved risks
- Decision owner
- Required future review trigger

### 5. Keep the Process Proportionate

- Not every fitting or drafting choice needs an entry.
- Record decisions that materially affect cost, reliability, operations, permitting, schedule, or future flexibility.

### 6. Engineering-Judgment Takeaway

- Engineering judgment is most valuable when its reasoning remains usable after the original engineer leaves the project.

## Drafting Task

Write **650–850 words covering Sections 1–3, 5, and 6**. Add the following example table:

| Decision | Reasoning worth preserving |
| --- | --- |
| Retain or eliminate a pipeline high point | Hydraulic performance, excavation risk, access and long-term ARV maintenance |
| Select a pump configuration | Design flows, system curve, firm capacity and future operating assumptions |
| Accept a material deviation | Applicable standard, performance basis, limitations and owner approval |
| Choose an HDD alignment | Geotechnical information, environmental impacts, constructability and contingency |
| Provide—or omit—future operational flexibility | Likelihood, incremental cost and consequence of later retrofit |

### Required Opening

> Twenty years from now, the drawings may clearly show what was built. They may say almost nothing about why it was designed that way.

### Required Closing

> A decision log does not replace calculations, drawings, or meeting minutes. It connects them by preserving the judgment that turned information into a decision.
