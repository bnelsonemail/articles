# Article Scope: The Project Kickoff Meeting Is the First Risk Workshop

## Topic

Use recent project-kickoff work to write about risk identification before design begins.

## Strong Angle

**“The Project Kickoff Meeting Is the First Risk Workshop”**

Kickoff meetings are often treated as introductions followed by schedule and administrative discussions. On a municipal infrastructure project, however, the kickoff may be the best opportunity to expose assumptions that could later affect permitting, design, cost, schedule, public coordination, and construction.

### Central Argument

> The earliest project risks usually appear first as unanswered questions, undocumented assumptions, and unclear ownership.

This article demonstrates practical engineering judgment while emphasizing business and project leadership—the direction now emphasized on devbybrice.com. ## Concise Outline

### 1. Why the Traditional Kickoff Falls Short

- Introductions, communication protocols, schedule, and deliverables are necessary.
- They do not automatically reveal the assumptions capable of disrupting the project.

### 2. Where Early Risks Hide

- Incomplete survey, SUE, geotechnical, record-drawing, or utility information
- Unconfirmed design criteria and operating conditions
- Permitting responsibilities and agency review durations
- Property, easement, access, and environmental constraints
- Undefined owner decisions and third-party dependencies
- Long-lead equipment and procurement assumptions

### 3. Convert Uncertainty into Ownership

- State the assumption.
- Identify what evidence will confirm it.
- Assign the person responsible.
- Establish the date by which the answer is needed.
- Record the consequence if it remains unresolved.

### 4. Separate Risks from Ordinary Action Items

- An action item says what must be done.
- A risk explains what may happen if it is not done—or if the underlying assumption proves wrong.

### 5. Carry the Results into Project Controls

- Risk and assumption register
- Design decision log
- Schedule constraints
- Cost contingencies
- Client decision tracker

### 6. Engineering-Judgment Takeaway

- A strong kickoff does not eliminate uncertainty.
- It prevents uncertainty from remaining invisible until it becomes expensive.

## Drafting Task

Write **650–850 words covering Sections 1–4 and 6** in one evening. Include the following table:

| Kickoff question | Risk being exposed |
| --- | --- |
| What owner-furnished information is still outstanding? | Design based on incomplete or incorrect conditions |
| Which criteria remain unconfirmed? | Rework after major design decisions |
| Which permits control the schedule? | Submittal or construction delay |
| Which decisions require third-party approval? | Dependency outside the project team’s control |
| What assumptions support the current budget? | Understated contingency or funding need |
| Who owns each unresolved item, and by when? | Risk recognized but never actively managed |

### Suggested Opening

> A project kickoff meeting should do more than introduce the team and review the schedule. It should expose the assumptions that can quietly become tomorrow’s change orders, delays, and client surprises.

### Required Closing

> The value of a kickoff is not measured by how smoothly the meeting runs. It is measured by how few important assumptions remain invisible afterward.
